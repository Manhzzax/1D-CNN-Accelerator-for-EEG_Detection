\section{Discussion}

The principal scientific question is whether a compact raw-EEG model can retain
its fixed-point decisions while providing measurable efficiency on an FPGA.
The present software result is deliberately not interpreted as clinical
superiority: its accuracy arises from balanced within-case windows, whereas
patient-independent and continuous-event protocols measure different forms of
generalization \cite{ali2024,gu2026}.

The design also separates neural storage from whole-system cost. A 10,030-B
tensor package does not include preprocessing, DMA buffers, processor control,
or acquisition hardware. Reporting these components and two timing boundaries
prevents an unfair comparison with a CPU, GPU, or another FPGA design.

Limitations include the offline preprocessing used to create the historical
reference, the within-case validation split, and the absence of an on-board
causal filtering frontend. These are explicit scope boundaries rather than
unreported assumptions.
